Embedded Systems Engineer, Fleet IoT Planet Express AM: Alex KiayaHM: Emily Carter

What's this job really about?

This engineer builds the software that runs on the small computers inside Planet Express trucks and trailers. Those devices track location, engine health, temperature and driver activity, then send the data back over cellular networks. The work means writing low-level code in C or C++ and making it reliable, low-power and secure in rough road conditions. It also includes updating devices remotely and working with the hardware and data teams on fleet tracking and the upcoming telematics rollout.

Screening questions

  1. Experience: “Tell me about an embedded device you've shipped. What microcontroller and RTOS did you use, and what part of the firmware did you own?” Listen for: hands-on C/C++ on ARM Cortex-M or similar, FreeRTOS or Zephyr, and code that actually shipped.
  2. Connectivity: “Have you worked with devices that send data over cellular (LTE-M, NB-IoT) or GPS/GNSS modules? How did you deal with lost connections?” Listen for: retries, buffering data while offline, and awareness of power use.
  3. Remote updates: “How have you handled firmware updates sent over the air to devices already in the field? What happens if an update fails halfway?” Listen for: fallback to the previous version, signed images and staged rollouts.
  4. Field debugging: “Describe a bug that only showed up on devices in the field, not on your desk. How did you track it down?” Listen for: logging and remote diagnostics, a scope or logic analyzer, and a clear, methodical approach.

RFID & Scanner Hardware Engineer Northstar AM: Alex KiayaHM: Sofia Chen

What's this job really about?

This engineer designs and tests the RFID readers and handheld scanners warehouse staff use to track every package. They pick antennas and chips, lay out circuit boards, and make sure the devices read tags reliably through cardboard, metal shelving and busy radio environments. They also work with suppliers to get new hardware certified and into production.

Screening questions

  1. Experience: “What RFID or barcode hardware have you designed or brought to production?” Listen for: UHF RFID, antenna design and work that went past the prototype stage.
  2. Read rates: “How have you improved read rates in a tough environment, like near metal or liquids?” Listen for: antenna tuning, field testing and measured results.
  3. Board design: “Which PCB tools do you use, and have you designed RF boards yourself?” Listen for: Altium or similar, impedance control and RF layout experience.
  4. Certification: “Have you taken a radio device through FCC certification?” Listen for: pre-compliance testing and working with test labs.
